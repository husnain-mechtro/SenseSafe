## PROJECT FACTS
- What it is: A proposal-stage IoT mechatronic prototype intended to monitor changes in LPG/combustible-gas response using an MQ-2 module and an ESP32-C3 SuperMini, provide local buzzer/LED warnings in the planned hardware build, show status on the integrated 0.42-inch OLED, and serve a simulated telemetry dashboard from the ESP32 over Wi-Fi.
- Context: Air University, Department of Mechatronics Engineering. Project team: Husnain Shaheen (2600847), Minahil Masood (2600838), Muhammad Waleed bin Mahmood (2600832). Section A. Supervisor: Umer Farooq. Document number ICT-MTS-SS-001. Revision 1.0. Date 22 November 2026.
- Build scope: One benchtop ESP32-C3 SuperMini with integrated 0.42-inch OLED, one MQ-2 gas sensor module, buzzer module, LED, 220-ohm LED resistor, breadboard, jumper wires, USB-C cable, and proposed 12 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider plus regulated 5 V supply. Proposal-stage dashboard data is simulated; physical sensor telemetry is not connected yet. Future commercial version would require a calibrated/selective sensor, enclosure, backup supply, diagnostics and appropriate certification.
- Key features: (1) MQ-2 analogue monitoring concept, (2) local audible/visual warning concept, (3) 0.42-inch OLED status display, (4) ESP32-hosted web portfolio and simulated telemetry dashboard.
- Hardware: ESP32-C3 SuperMini with 0.42-inch OLED; MQ-2 module powered from regulated 5 V; proposed analogue output divider 12 kΩ from MQ-2 AO to ADC node and 10 kΩ from ADC node to GND; proposed ADC GPIO0 / ADC1_CH0; buzzer control proposed on GPIO3 (driver and polarity must be confirmed); LED proposed on GPIO4 through 220 Ω; common OLED variant is SSD1306-compatible 72×40 I²C with SDA GPIO5, SCL GPIO6, address 0x3C, but exact board revision must be verified. Web interface is served by ESP32 WebServer from LittleFS; browser uses HTTP GET /api/telemetry and GET /api/demo?state=normal|warning|fault. The dashboard and firmware are simulation-only for this proposal stage.
- Target market / region: Pakistan, initially domestic kitchens and small commercial environments; parts intended to be locally available.
- Known limitations and corrections: The original component list named Arduino UNO R4 WiFi, but the selected controller is ESP32-C3 SuperMini OLED. MQ-2 is broad-response and cross-sensitive, not LPG-selective; raw ADC values are not ppm. MQ-2 warm-up and calibration are required. Never connect a potentially 5 V AO signal directly to the 3.3 V ESP32-C3 ADC. The proposed 12 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider converts 5 V to about 2.27 V. The OLED pin mapping is common to this board family but must be checked on the exact revision. This is an educational prototype, not a certified life-safety detector.
